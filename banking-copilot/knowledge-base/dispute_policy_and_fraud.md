# Bank Customer Policy: Card Dispute & Fraud Resolution

## 1. Scope and Eligibility
Customers may file a dispute for transactions appearing on their checking, savings, or credit card accounts if:
- The transaction was unauthorized or fraudulent.
- The amount charged differs from the agreed receipt/invoice.
- A recurring subscription was billed after verified cancellation.
- Purchased goods or services were never delivered, damaged, or significantly not as described.

## 2. Timelines for Filing
- **Debit Card & Checking Disputes (Regulation E)**: Notice must be received within **60 calendar days** from the date the periodic statement showing the unauthorized transaction was sent.
- **Credit Card Disputes (Regulation Z)**: Disputes must be submitted within **60 calendar days** of the billing statement date.

## 3. Investigation Process and Provisional Credit
- **Initial Review**: The bank acknowledges the dispute claim within **24 hours** and issues a unique Case ID (e.g., `DSP-XXXXX`).
- **Provisional Credit**: For qualifying debit card claims, if the investigation takes more than 10 business days, a provisional credit will be posted to the customer's account while research continues.
- **Merchant Response**: The merchant is given **30 calendar days** to provide evidence (receipts, proof of delivery, signed vouchers).
- **Final Resolution**: Maximum total investigation time is **45 to 90 days** depending on transaction origin (domestic vs. cross-border).

## 4. Emergency Fraud Mitigation
If a customer suspects active account takeover or card cloning:
1. Immediately execute a temporary card freeze via the mobile app or digital banking assistant.
2. If physical possession of the card is lost, mark status as **PERMANENTLY_BLOCKED** to trigger an automatic contactless card reissue (3–5 business days, or 24-hour rush delivery upon request).
3. Transfer critical cases directly to the 24/7 Priority Fraud Operations Desk.
